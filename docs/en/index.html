The game

You run, double-jump and clamber over a city made of cardboard. The camera follows GIG from the side like an operator on a film set: it closes in when the Esattori arrive, cranes up when the view opens out, and turns with the street.

  1. 1

    Jump

    Tombs, rooftops, scaffolding, air purifiers. Anything that will hold a fifty-year-old man is a platform.

  2. 2

    Disarm

    The Esattori, SIDERA's repo men, are people from the neighbourhood, hired by the day, with an arm on lease. You don't take them down: you take them off the leash.

  3. 3

    Unlock

    The cyber arm opens what SIDERA has locked. Every one of its functions used to be a paid extra: GIG takes them back, one at a time.

Under the red cloud

At San Brunone cemetery the white headstones don't stay white: anyone from the Tamburi district has always known that. By 2087 the steelworks has been cold for more than sixty years, sealed under white domes. The red cloud is still there. SIDERA calls it “clean-up steam”.

For generations Taranto had to choose between work and health. SIDERA found the answer: it rents out both. The air is called ARIA+ (aria means air). The arm, for those hurt on the job, is called MANODOPERA (“workforce”). Miss a payment and it's switched off remotely. Then the Esattori come.

By 2087 almost everything in Taranto has changed. Not the rites of Holy Week. And someone still writes on the walls.

How we got here

  1. 2026

    The blast furnaces go out. A company called SIDERA buys the plant and promises a clean-up.

  2. 2041

    White domes rise above the plant. On the domes it says “Construction site, no entry”. The belts that used to carry the ore are rebuilt and raised on pylons, from the port to the domes: people call it the Linea. On its side it says “soil to be cleaned up”.

  3. 2065

    SIDERA gives a new arm to workers who were hurt on the job. It is called MANODOPERA. An engineer from the Tamburi invented it.

  4. 2067

    The arm moves to a monthly subscription. The clean-up workers also wear the sleeve, on their good arm. “Shift mode” arrives. The ad says: night-time physiotherapy, while you sleep.

  5. 2087

    SIDERA has set the date: on Easter Sunday the end of the clean-up will be celebrated. “Taranto is pure.”

The air

The air is called ARIA+ and you pay for it in instalments, like streaming series. The white and mint-green towers belong to it. Above the cloud, for those who can afford it, there is ARIA+ High Altitude.

The Colombe (the Doves) are white, carry the ARIA+ logo and measure the air. They lie. When the mistral carries the dust over the Tamburi, the council announces a Wind Day. The Colombe say no, and spray the scent of clean.

GIG

Cataldo Gigante, 50, from the Tamburi.

A conveyor belt on the Linea took his left arm. He built the new one himself, in his mother's garage, and then built one for half the city. When Mimmo, his childhood friend, died, he asked too many questions: within a week SIDERA had taken his job and his house, and struck his name off the patents. And switched off his arm.

Gruff, wry and stubborn. He can fix anything except his own life.

He wears the ochre shirt of his father Vito, a crane operator at the port. They built the first arm together, in the garage. Vito used to say: “You've got one hand. Make yourself the other.”

As a boy he had the best left in the Tamburi. Then the Linea took his left.

He won't get there alone. You'll meet the others along the way.

Mimmo

His childhood friend, a crane operator too. Arm number 2 is his: GIG built it for him after the accident at the port. He died on the night of the dead, 2086, in his sleep. For SIDERA, it was a sudden illness.

Addolorata

His mother. A seamstress, seventy-five years old, she calls him Catà. The garage is hers. At fifty, GIG moved back in with her.

Tere

The voice of arm number 1. GIG put her there in 2064. Since 2067 she has been silent.

Totò

Eleven years old, asthmatic. On windy days he can't go out. He writes REVENGE on the walls. He is the one telling this story: “I was eleven that night. I'll tell it the way I remember it. With a few extra colours.”

The Esattori

They repossess the arms of anyone who misses a payment. They are people from the neighbourhoods, paid per repossession: the rider with the backpack, the collector in the hi-vis bib, the hired hand, the bailiff with the writ in hand.

SIDERA

It has no face. It has the posters, the screens, the towers, the Colombe. It only speaks through press releases.

Holy Thursday, 3 pm

17 April 2087 is Holy Thursday. At three in the afternoon the hooded penitents set out from the Carmine church, barefoot. The sky is hazy, and the graves of San Brunone are red with dust.

GIG is standing in front of Mimmo's headstone. “Hi, Mimmo. I brought you your coffee.”

On the night of the dead in 2086 the cemetery was full of votive lights. SIDERA said Mimmo had died in his sleep. The log of his arm said something else: seven hours of work, under the domes, restricted sector. Return to residence at 5:50. Holder not detected. Subscription suspended.

GIG asked questions. In a week he lost his job, his home and his name on the patents. His arm, number 1, has been switched off ever since.

Now the Esattori are at the gate, and a bailiff with a notice to read out. “Dear former associate Gigante Cataldo, SIDERA thanks you for choosing MANODOPERA.”

“I invented it.”

“Not on record.”

It starts to rain. GIG runs between the graves and through the streets of the Tamburi, with an arm that doesn't respond.

In his mother's garage Vito's keyboard is still there. Number 1 is a prototype, with a service port. Three keys.

The arm comes back on. The right eye, the one the company used to send him ads through, turns cyan. A voice he hasn't heard in twenty years says: “Twenty years switched off, Catà. What did I miss?”

“Nothing good.”

“Tonight I'm going to see where the arms go.”

The prologue ends here. It is what you play in the demo.

The city

San Brunone

The cemetery is right up against the plant. The white headstones don't stay white.

The Tamburi

The district under the Linea. Washing on the lines, air purifiers on the balconies, torn posters pasted one over another.

The Linea

The belts on their pylons, from the port to the domes. They pass over the last street of the district.

The domes

White, sealed. Inside, they say, is the clean-up.

Holy Week

The hooded penitents in pairs, barefoot. The troccola (a wooden rattle) instead of bells, which are tied during those days. In 2087 almost everything in Taranto has changed. Not this.

The demo

The prologue is already playable: the cemetery and the gate. This is a work-in-progress demo from 18 November 2026, not the finished game.

  • The levels are still grey boxes. The cardboard set is on its way. The characters, though, are already the puppets.
  • There are no cutscenes yet, and the electric water is harmless, for now.
  • 64-bit Windows. Best with a gamepad: the controls are in the README file inside the zip.
  • Windows may warn that the program is not recognised: it is an unsigned executable.
  • It is not for sale and has no release date.

How it's made, honestly

  • It is a video game, made in Unreal Engine 5, that imitates stop motion: the characters are 3D models that move in jerky steps like puppets.
  • Some of the tools are artificial intelligence: ChatGPT, Meshy, HeyGen, Suno.
  • It is a personal project. It is not for sale and has no release date.

Revenge

Ctrl-Alt-Revenge is a work of fiction. SIDERA, ARIA+, MANODOPERA and all the characters are made up, and any resemblance to real people or companies is coincidental. The places and rituals of Taranto are real, and we treat them with respect.
